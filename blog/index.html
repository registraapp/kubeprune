12 AWS cost cuts that don't need a re-architecture

Most AWS savings that don't need a redesign come from the same twelve places: oversized resources and pod requests, non-production running 24/7, orphaned volumes, NAT gateway traffic to S3, gp2 volumes, missing Savings Plans, no Spot, x86 by default, S3 without lifecycle rules, logs kept forever, unused public IPv4 addresses, and EKS clusters on extended support. Each one can be found with a few commands.

Amazon EKS

The real cost of EKS extended support

On Amazon EKS, a cluster in standard support costs $0.10 per hour. Fourteen months after its version is released on EKS, it moves to extended support at $0.60 per hour, automatically and by default: about $438 a month instead of $73, or $4,380 more per cluster per year. After twelve more months, AWS upgrades the control plane on its own, with no notice and no rollback.

Amazon EKS

EKS upgrade checklist: from 1.31 to 1.35 without breakage

An EKS upgrade goes one minor version at a time: inventory deprecated APIs and upgrade insights, align nodes, upgrade the control plane, then nodes and add-ons, and repeat for the next hop. Since July 2026, EKS can roll back to the previous minor version within 7 days, with conditions. For several hops in a row or a critical cluster, a blue/green cluster is still the safest net.
